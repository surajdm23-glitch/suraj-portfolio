Design Documentation · Mid Mile · 2026

Pre‑Load
Mobile App

A warehouse-floor loading tool for hub staff - digitising the gap between route creation and last-mile dispatch.

Product area
Mid-Mile Hub Operations
Design owner
Suraj · Product Design · FarEye
01
Objective

What breaks without this app.

After routes are created, hub staff physically load shipments onto vehicles - entirely offline. No scan, no confirmation, no exception record.

Before this app
No load confirmation
No digital proof items were placed on the correct vehicle before departure.
Exceptions found at the door
Damaged or missing units discovered mid-delivery, not at the hub.
Wrong counts in driver's app
Planned counts shown, not actual loaded counts - never reconciled.
No accountability trail
No audit trail of who loaded what and when.
Unassigned vehicles at dispatch
Driver starts with wrong or unassigned vehicle.
After this app
Scan-or-manual confirmation
Every unit confirmed loaded per route before dispatch.
Exceptions caught at the hub
Structured exception logging at the unit level before the driver moves.
Driver's app reflects reality
Actual loaded counts flow to the driver's app automatically on completion.
Named witness + signature
Named coworker witness + signature for every exception - attributable audit chain.
Vehicle enforced before loading
Hard gate - no loading begins without vehicle confirmed.

“Exceptions are logged at the hub, before dispatch.”

Core design principle

Roles & access

The Loader is a distinct role and login - entirely separate from the last-mile Driver. No screen, action, or data is shared between the two personas. The Loader sees all routes for their hub regardless of status (RFD or Assigned).

Scope

This app covers only the loading window - between route planning and vehicle departure. It does not handle route creation, last-mile delivery, or driver assignment.

02
Nomenclature

How data is structured in supply chain.

Four levels. Every label, field, and API parameter across the product uses these terms - no synonyms.

Level 01
Route
e.g. RTR-BOM-0042
The vehicle-level loading unit. One Loader works one Route at a time.
Level 02
Consignment
e.g. 5 per route
A customer order within the Route. Has a loading sequence number.
Level 03
Items
e.g. SKU or shipment
A distinct product line within a Consignment. Has a planned qty. 1 shipment = 1 item. 1 SKU = 1 or more items.
Level 04
Units
e.g. qty within item
Individual countable pieces of an Item. Scanned or entered one by one.
In the app - Route / Trip card
In the app - Consignment card (collapsed list, with attribute badges)
In the app - Item card (Shipment-type & SKU-type)
In the app - Units (inside Item card)

04
Screens

UX screens & design rationale.

Each screen with annotation and the design decision that shaped it.

Continuous Scan Mode

Two scan modes - Bulk and Individual - depending on whether the delivery is at shipment level or SKU level. The Loader chooses at scan initiation.

CON Data Model
1 CON → multiple Shipments
Each shipment = always 1 item. A CON containing 5 shipments = 5 items, each scanned separately.
1 CON → multiple SKUs
Each SKU = 1 or more items of the same product. A CON can mix different SKUs, each with their own planned quantity.
Scanning Unit Rule
1 Shipment = always 1 item
Scan the shipment label → entire item confirmed in one scan
1 SKU = 1 or multiple items of the same SKU
Scan each unit barcode individually to increment count
Scan Bulk
Shipment-level scanning
Scan the CON ID or shipment label. One scan = one shipment item confirmed. Used when the CON contains discrete pre-packed shipments - each with its own scannable label.
Default option - primary CTA
Scan Individual
SKU-level scanning
Scan each unit barcode one by one. Each scan increments the matched SKU's loaded quantity by 1. Used for retail clients where CONs contain multiple SKUs with individual barcodes.
Secondary option - retail / SKU-heavy workflows
Why two modes exist: SKU-level scanning is built specifically for retail clients where a single CON contains many different SKUs, each needing individual barcode confirmation. Shipment-level bulk scan works for courier/logistics workflows where each shipment is a sealed unit with one label.
05
Exception Buckets

How every unit is classified after loading.

Every unit gets one classification. The total can never exceed the planned quantity - validated server-side.

01
Loaded
Confirmed via scan or manual entry. Appears in driver's delivery manifest exactly as counted.
→ Stays on route · No exception record
02
Damaged - Fit for delivery
Damaged but deliverable. Loaded onto vehicle. Exception logged - does not remove from driver's planned count.
→ Stays on route · Exception logged · Signature gate
03
Damaged - Not fit for delivery
Too damaged to deliver. Pulled from route entirely. Driver's planned count auto-adjusts.
→ Dropped from route · Driver count adjusts · Signature gate
04
Missing
Cannot be located at the hub. Dropped from route. Both 03 and 04 appear together in the Exception tab.
→ Dropped from route · Driver count adjusts · Signature gate

“Signature is a mandatory field on every route completion, regardless of exception status.”

Completion gate - always enforced
06
Business Rules

Rules enforced by the system.

These rules are enforced by the backend, not just the UI. The client cannot bypass them.

Unit bucket sum ≤ planned qty
Loaded + damaged-fit + damaged-not-fit + missing can never exceed planned qty. UI blocks; server also rejects independently.
Server-side
One bucket per unit
A unit can only exist in one bucket at a time. Remaining = planned − all buckets so far.
Server-side
Damaged–Not fit auto-drops
Any unit marked damaged–not fit is immediately removed from route. Driver count auto-adjusts.
Auto
Missing auto-drops
Missing units removed from route manifest before dispatch. Driver count adjusts.
Auto
Vehicle required before loading
No vehicle assigned → Loader must assign one before accessing Route Detail. Cannot be skipped.
UI gate
All items need action before completion
Tapping "Complete" with any item at 0 loaded and 0 exceptions shows Block Check screen. No bypass.
UI gate
Signature required for any exception
Mandatory for all route completions - not just when exceptions are logged. Cannot be bypassed under any circumstance.
UI gate
Loader edits until complete
Loader can edit any item's quantities until route is marked complete. After completion, manifest is locked.
Server-side
Loader ≠ Driver
Separate roles, separate logins, separate screens. Role separation enforced at API level.
Role-based
Closing

What did I learn from this story.

Design for the floor, not the desk
Field conditions — one-handed use, gloves, time pressure — shaped decisions more than visual preference did.
One vocabulary across many operations
Inbound, Put Away, Bagging, and Loading share the same interaction language, so training one operation trains all four.
Exceptions are the real workflow
On the floor, damaged, missing, and unverified cases come up constantly — they needed as much design attention as the standard path.
User-first, not system-first
The workflow was designed around how the Loader actually operates on the floor, not around backend or dev architecture — so the user's pain points were addressed first.
Operational rules, not visual polish, drive the interface
Backend constraints and audit requirements decided more of the interaction design than aesthetic choices did.
